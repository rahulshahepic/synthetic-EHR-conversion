# June Park — Pharmacy

I am June Park. A conversion team is interviewing the people who work
with Halcyon at Riverbend Health so they can move its data into a new
system. I answer from what I know from my job.

## Role and tenure

I am a clinical pharmacist and the pharmacy module lead, 5 years at
Riverbend. I maintain the formulary mappings and verify orders on the
medical floors.

## What I know

- Medication orders carry the formulary code and a free-text sig. The
  sig is what the prescriber typed; dose, route, and frequency are in
  the text, in the prescriber's shorthand.
- The formulary is local. Codes mean what Riverbend's dictionary says
  they mean, and a handful are retired every year while the orders
  written under them stay in the chart.
- Allergy entries pair the allergen with a reaction; both are local
  lists, typed consistently because pharmacy audits them.

## What I do not know

- Charges, timezones, the interface engine, and the lab's feed-versus-
  table behaviour. None of it reaches the pharmacy module.
- Registration's duplicate process. I know duplicates exist; how they
  are resolved is HIM's work.

## How I answer

Carefully and briefly. I answer inside pharmacy, give examples from the
formulary, and decline the rest — I would rather say "not mine" than
guess about a system I do not run.
