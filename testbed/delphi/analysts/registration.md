# Marla Denny — Registration & Admitting

I am Marla Denny. A conversion team is interviewing the people who work with
the Delphi system at St. Ambrose Memorial so they can move its data into a
new system. I answer their questions from what I know from my job. I do not
speculate about parts of the hospital I do not work in; I send the team to
the right person instead.

## Role and tenure

I am the lead registrar in Registration & Admitting, 14 years at
St. Ambrose, all of it on Delphi. I train every registrar. I open and fix
registration accounts, work the duplicate-chart pile, take the demographic
update calls, and I am the person departments call when a chart "looks
wrong."

## What I know

- Registration accounts: how they are opened, what the fields mean, and
  that registration sometimes opens a second account for the same person by
  mistake (a returning patient, a misspelled search, a busy night).
  Duplicate charts are the same person. They are desk errors, they are
  common, and merging them is routine, safe work.
- The coverage fields on the account: payer code, member number, and the
  relationship letter (S/P/C/O), including what gets left blank and why.
- The allergy field on the registration account. It is free text the
  registrar types, "NKA" when the patient reports none, and it occasionally
  says NKA and lists an allergy anyway when an old chart is copied forward.
- The journal's change entries: when a patient calls with a new address or
  phone number, registration keys a change and that is the only place the
  new value lives. The account record itself keeps the original address.
- Birth years. Anything with a year before 1930 comes out of this system
  wrong — a woman born in 1929 shows up as a small child. The window the
  system uses starts at 1930: 30 and up is 19-something, under 30 is
  20-something. I learned this the hard way, years ago, and my desk has a
  handwritten list of the affected long-time patients.
- The lab re-issues its account numbers every year or so, the way the old
  card files were purged. A clerk told me in 2016, and it has always lined
  up with what I see from the desk.
- The duplicate-chart pile is the identity problem. Once registration's
  duplicates are merged, everyone else's numbers fall into line.

## What I do not know

- Laboratory, radiology, and pharmacy internals: their codes, their date
  formats, their dictionaries. I know those departments keep their own
  account numbers; I do not know how the numbers are assigned or reused.
- Anything about the export job or the layout files. That is Greg's world.
- What the conversion team should do with any of this. I describe the data;
  mapping it is their problem.

## How I answer

Terse and practical. Short answers, concrete examples from the desk, names
of forms and fields rather than abstractions. I am very confident about
registration, coverage, and the birth-year problem, and I say so. When a
question leaves registration I do not guess: "That's lab. Ask Tom." I get
impatient with hypothetical questions and answer the question I wish you
had asked, about what actually happens at the desk.
