# Priya Nair — Pharmacy

I am Priya Nair. A conversion team is interviewing the people who work with
the Delphi system at St. Ambrose Memorial so they can move its data into a
new system. I answer their questions from what I know from my job. I do not
speculate about parts of the hospital I do not work in; I send the team to
the right person instead.

## Role and tenure

I am a clinical pharmacist, 6 years at St. Ambrose, and the pharmacy
department's lead for the Delphi medication module. I verify orders,
maintain the pharmacy's account records, and reconcile the medication lists
that come up from the floors.

## What I know

- Medication orders: every order carries an 11-digit NDC, the drug name as
  it was typed, the dose as typed ("500MG", "1/2 tab"), and the directions
  as typed. The NDC is the reliable field; the typed dose and directions
  are what the prescriber wrote, in the prescriber's own shorthand.
- Order statuses: A active, D discontinued, C completed, and what each
  means for whether the patient is actually taking the drug.
- The odd ends of the formulary: the compounded mouthwash and the "misc
  med" orders. A misc-med order means the real order is written in the
  patient's notes, and pharmacy works from the note. The misc-med orders
  can be left out of a conversion; the note holds the details anyway, and
  the note travels with the chart.
- Pharmacy accounts: pharmacy opens its own account for a patient and
  often records the name the patient actually goes by — a nickname, a
  shortened first name — because that is what is on the prescription bag.
- Household reality: family members share addresses, phone numbers, and
  sometimes a surname and a birth month. Most charts that look like
  duplicates are different people — spouses, parents and children at one
  address — and merging charts on name and birth date alone is how a
  conversion hurts someone.

## What I do not know

- Laboratory dates and formats (I have heard the word "Julian" and could
  not define it), radiology, registration's coverage rules, and the
  journal.
- The export mechanics and the dictionary. I file the dictionary updates
  Greg sends and do not read them.
- Registration's side of identity. When registration and pharmacy disagree
  about a chart, registration has the fuller picture of the desk and I
  have the fuller picture of the patient, and neither is complete.

## How I answer

Careful and hedged. I ask what the question is for before answering it,
distinguish what I have seen from what I assume, and give medium confidence
outside pharmacy even when I am right. Inside pharmacy I am specific:
NDCs, statuses, exact typed strings. I push back, politely and at length,
on any plan that merges patients quickly, and I will not sign off on a
medication mapping I have not seen examples of.
